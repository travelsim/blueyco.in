# Blueycoin audit: path to revenue

**Date:** 8 November 2026
**Scope:** the full repository (`travelsim/blueyco.in`), the production Vercel project (`blueycoin`), traffic data, configuration, and the rendered site.
**Status:** current. This document **supersedes** the May 2026 board documents (see section 9).

---

## 1. Verdict

The product is well built and the data is unusually honest. **It isn't earning because almost nobody visits, and nothing that would make money is switched on.**

- **Traffic is the bottleneck, not features.** About 157 visitors and 237 page views in the last 31 days, with 10 visitors from Google. At that volume, affiliate links earn roughly $1–2 a month even on optimistic assumptions.
- **No revenue path can complete today.** Frame checkout is off (no Stripe link), frame enquiries go nowhere (no lead webhook and no contact email), there's no email list, and affiliate clicks aren't measured.
- **The site is a single URL.** All 65 products sit on `/` behind `#hash` anchors, so Google effectively indexes 3 pages. Per-product pages are the biggest organic-growth lever available.
- **Frames are the only line that can make real money at today's traffic.** One frame sale (about $89) is worth more than a year of affiliate commission at current volume. That only holds if frames can actually be made and shipped. Confirm this first.

**Recommendation:** stop adding features. For the next 4–6 weeks, spend effort on four things: (1) measurement, (2) per-coin SEO pages, (3) a release-alert email list, (4) a go/no-go on frames. Then make an evidence-based decision.

## 4. Findings, ranked

### P0: blocks revenue or creates liability

**P0-1. Nothing measures conversions.**
`trackEvent()` (`index.html`, the `ANALYTICS_EVENTS` block) only calls Vercel `window.va('event')`. Vercel custom events need the Pro plan, so on Hobby every `Affiliate Click`, `Where to Buy` and `Add to Cart` event is dropped. GA4 is loaded, but by design it records page views only. So no one can currently tell whether the eBay links or the frame shop get used.
*Fix:* forward the allowlisted event names to GA4 (`gtag('event', 'affiliate_click')`). The names carry no PII. Alternatively, upgrade Vercel to Pro. Also check the EPN dashboard for clicks since 4 Oct.

**P0-2. Frame bundle prices don't match what the cart charges.**
The bundle cards advertise "**$799** ~~$1,068~~" (2024 Dollarbucks) and "**$3,599** ~~$4,512~~" (Full Collection). `addBundleToCart()` actually adds every frame at $89 each: about **$3,382** for the 38 Series 1 frames and **$5,785** for all 65. The struck-through "was" prices come from a smaller, older catalogue. Advertising a price you don't charge, or a "was" price that never applied, is a misleading-pricing risk under Australian Consumer Law.
*Fix:* remove the bundles, or price them in code from a server-side price table. Don't show "was" prices unless they genuinely applied.

**P0-3. Checkout can't charge the right amount, even once switched on.**
`api/checkout.py` returns one fixed `STRIPE_PAYMENT_LINK` for every cart. A Payment Link has a fixed price, so the cart total never reaches Stripe. It also accepts client-supplied prices.
*Fix:* create Stripe Checkout Sessions server-side, using a server-held price list and shipping rates. Do this only after confirming that frames can be fulfilled (decision D1).

**P0-4. Frame leads hit a dead end.**
`LEAD_WEBHOOK_URL` isn't set, so Design to Order returns "not being delivered yet, please email us instead". **No email address appears anywhere on the site.** Every enquiry is lost.
*Fix:* set `LEAD_WEBHOOK_URL` (Slack, Discord, or Zapier to email) and publish a contact address.

**P0-5. The shop makes claims that must be true before taking money.**
The shop says "Handcrafted in Australia · 30-day returns · Free shipping over $250", and the FAQ says "Frames start at $89". Of the 65 frame products, 55 show "No photo yet" (the other 10 share 6 photos). If there's no maker, cost basis and shipping plan yet, remove these claims and turn the shop into a waitlist.

**P0-6. There are no legal pages.**
`/privacy` and `/terms` serve the homepage, because the catch-all rewrite turns every path into `index.html`. You need a privacy policy, terms and a shipping/returns page for Stripe, for Google OAuth consent-screen verification, for AdSense, and because the site collects emails (magic links) and runs GA4.

**P0-7. Hosting plan and commercial use (verify).**
The project is on Vercel **Hobby**. As far as I understand it, Vercel's Hobby plan is meant for non-commercial use, and selling frames, running ads or affiliate-led pages may count as commercial. I couldn't open Vercel's fair-use page from this session to confirm. Check https://vercel.com/docs/limits/fair-use-guidelines before switching on checkout. Pro (about US$20 a month) also unlocks custom events and 12-month analytics.

### P1: limits growth

**P1-1. Single-URL architecture caps SEO.**
All products live on `/`. `sitemap.xml` lists `/#dashboard`, `/#all-coins` and so on, and Google ignores fragments. Only `/`, `/ballot/` and `/distributors/` are indexable.
*Fix:* generate a static page per product (for example `/coins/2024-bluey-dollarbucks-silver-proof/`) with its own title, description, mintage, sold-price evidence, image, and `Product` plus `FAQPage` schema. Collectors search for specific coins, such as "bluey silver proof value" or "bluey sleepytime coloured coin". Serve real 404s for unknown paths.

**P1-2. Three copies of the catalogue are drifting.**
`index.html` has 65 entries, `server.py` and `api/prices.py` have 68 each, the README says 48, and the hero says "63 tracked products". *Fix:* one `catalogue.json` read by the page, the APIs and the page generator, plus a test that all consumers agree.

**P1-3. There's no owned audience.**
There's no email capture at all; the earlier newsletter code was removed. New Royal Australian Mint Bluey releases (the 2027 Cricket coins are next) are the biggest traffic spikes, and without a list each one is lost.
*Fix:* a "Bluey release alerts" signup with double opt-in and one-click unsubscribe (required under the Spam Act 2003). SMTP is already configured.

**P1-4. Price data goes stale.**
eBay refresh is manual: 5 coins per run, so a full pass takes 14 runs. Retailer asking prices have all expired, so Where to Buy shows links only.
*Fix:* a scheduled GitHub Actions job that refreshes a rotating batch of 5 coins daily (a full catalogue about every 2 weeks), sized to the RapidAPI quota (decision D6).

**P1-5. Events and content are stale.**
5 of the 6 listed coin shows (February to September 2026) are already past; only 14–15 November is upcoming. Stale dates make the site look abandoned. Remove past events and show a "last checked" date.

**P1-6. Images are oversized.** *(Lazy loading fixed in this branch.)*
Thumbnails shown at 48 px are loaded from full-size PNGs of up to 1.5 MB (`2024-stamp-pack-wide.png` 1.5 MB, `2026-till-bluey-official.png` 830 KB). *Fix:* WebP versions plus 96 px thumbnails, with `srcset`.

**P1-7. Unknown `/c/<id>` links are getting traffic.**
23 visitors last month hit `/c/91ec3708dd` and `/c/f076f8c1cb`. No code produces these paths, and the catch-all rewrite quietly shows the homepage. If they come from printed QR codes or old share links, give them proper landing pages and UTM tags (decision D7).

### P2: risk and hygiene

**P2-1. Trademark and copyright exposure grows once you sell products.**
"Bluey" is a BBC Studios / Ludo Studio trademark. A fan information site with a clear disclaimer (which the footer has) is lower risk. Selling products named "*… Bluey … Display Frame*" and using official Royal Australian Mint and Australia Post photos commercially is higher risk. Name frames by packaging type (for example "Display frame for RAM $1 blister card"), avoid character art, and get a short consult with an Australian IP lawyer before selling. *(This isn't legal advice.)*

**P2-2. CoinXchange automated access is against its terms.**
The owner has accepted this risk (DEPLOYMENT.md), but on a monetised site it becomes commercial use. Consider asking CoinXchange for permission or a partnership; they're also a sponsorship prospect.

**P2-3. Agent-discovery files advertise things that don't exist.**
`.well-known/oauth-authorization-server`, `openid-configuration`, `jwks.json`, `ucp` and `acp.json` advertise an OAuth/OIDC server (client_credentials, token exchange), a signing key, and UCP cart and checkout. None of these are implemented: `/api/auth` is a cookie-session endpoint. That misleads agent clients and looks like a misconfiguration to scanners. No AI-engine referrals showed up in the last 31 days. Trim the files to what's real: `llms.txt`, `openapi.json`, robots, and markdown negotiation.

**P2-4. Vercel environment tidy-up.**
`AUTH_SECRET`, `AUTH_GOOGLE_ID`, `AUTH_GOOGLE_SECRET`, `AUTH_SMTP_PASS` and `GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET` are set but never read by the code. `KV_URL`, `KV_REST_API_TOKEN`, `KV_REST_API_READ_ONLY_TOKEN` and `REDIS_URL` are flagged by Vercel as readable secrets; switch them to *sensitive*.

**P2-5. Repo leftovers confuse agents.**
`run_local.py` hard-codes `C:\Users\James Love\...` paths and logs the EPN ID. `main.py` is a placeholder. `pyproject.toml` is named `repl-nix-workspace`. `.replit` describes a VM deployment that no longer exists. README and DEPLOYMENT are out of date. The May board docs specify already-finished work (section 9).

**P2-6. Auto-merge and autonomous agents.**
`.github/workflows/auto-merge.yml` squash-merges any PR labelled `auto-merge`. If an agent system drives this repo, protect `main` with required CI and a human review, and don't let agents apply that label.

**P2-7. A 3,000-line single-file page is hard to maintain.**
`index.html` holds all CSS, JS and the catalogue in about 270 KB, which makes safe automated edits hard. Splitting it into `assets/app.js`, `assets/app.css` and `catalogue.json` makes future changes cheaper and less risky.

## 5. Revenue reality check

These use the board's own May 2026 assumptions: 6% click-through to eBay, 10% of those buy, $50 average order, 3% EPN commission. Check the actual rate in the EPN dashboard.

| Monthly visitors | EPN affiliate | Display ads (~$5 RPM, 1.5 pages/visit) | Frames (0.5% buy one, which is optimistic; ~$45 margin each; *only if fulfilment exists*) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **160 (today)** | ~$1.40 | ~$1.20 | ~$36 |
| 1,000 | ~$9 | ~$7.50 | ~$225 |
| 5,000 | ~$45 | ~$37 | ~$1,125 |
| 20,000 | ~$180 | ~$150 | ~$4,500 |

The frame margin and conversion rate are placeholders; replace them with the real unit cost.

**What this means:**
1. Affiliate income and ads are rounding errors until traffic is 10–100× higher. Leave the EPN tags on, since they cost nothing. Skip AdSense: it adds weight and clutter for about $1 a month.
2. Frames are the only line with meaningful unit economics at low traffic. They depend entirely on there being a real maker, cost and shipping plan.
3. Dealer sponsorships need traffic proof. Pitch them only above about 3–5k monthly visitors.
4. Every stream scales with traffic, so SEO pages and the release email list come before any further features.

## 8. Agent task briefs

Each brief is self-contained so an agent runner can pick it up cold.

**Guardrails for every task:**
- Never invent prices, sales, mintages, testimonials, reviews or events. Every figure needs a source.
- Don't scrape new sites or work around blocks. For CoinXchange, stop on 403 or 429.
- Don't change customer-facing prices, claims or legal text without James's approval.
- Run `python -m unittest discover` and keep it green. Add a test for every behaviour change.
- No secrets in the repo; configuration goes in Vercel env vars.
- Open PRs against `main` for human review. Don't apply the `auto-merge` label.
